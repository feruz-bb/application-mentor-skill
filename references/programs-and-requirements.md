# Programs, Requirements and Deadlines

**How to use this file.** Everything marked `[D]` is durable structure — the shape of a process, which stays true for years. Everything marked `[V]` is volatile — a number or date that goes stale, several of which already have. Give the student the `[D]` pattern so they know what to expect, then send them to the official page for the `[V]` figure. Never let a decision rest on a remembered number.

## Is this program real?

Run this before helping anyone write an application to a program that charges a fee. A beautifully written application to a fake program is wasted work, and the corpus is emphatic about how common these have become: a group of four or five people rents a venue somewhere, runs a two-day "program," and issues certificates with no institutional standing. Adverts promise "fully funded places" that in practice go to the organisers' own circle; everyone else is offered self-funded.

**Green flags:** a real institutional host (a university, UNICEF, American Councils, Erasmus/EU, Rotary, a government agency); an official email that answers substantive questions; no application fee at all — Erasmus charges none, Kectil and Aspire Leaders are free; a plausible proportion of funded places; a certificate that means something to someone other than the issuer; a formal visa-support process.

**Red flags:** an application fee for a "fully funded" opportunity; a token number of funded seats (5–10) against a large intake; "guaranteed acceptance" tiers sold without selection; a promise of a Schengen invitation letter within 24 hours; an organiser registered nowhere identifiable; awards and medals distributed by private companies rather than universities or academies.

**The three-rule test** if the student still wants to apply to a fee-charging program:
1. Email every question to the official address in the announcement. **A real program replies.**
2. If their portfolio is not strong, expect to land in the self-funded category — do not pay to discover this.
3. If only ~5–10 fully funded places exist, do not apply.

**Bought awards.** The scheme: submit an article of any quality, pay 600,000–900,000 UZS in "conference expenses," receive a medal in the post. Tell students plainly what the corpus concludes: *"bu ko'krak nishoni va diplom sizga na davlat stipendiyasini, na xalqaro grantlarni yutishda yordam beradi."* It does not help a state scholarship or an international grant, and experienced reviewers recognise the issuers.

**Headline grant numbers.** "Uzbek student wins $400,000 scholarship" — this is a tuition and living-cost waiver over four years, moving between the university's own accounts. Only the stipend portion, a small fraction, ever reaches the student. Correct this expectation early; it changes which offers a student should accept.

## Scholarship types `[D]`

- **Fully funded** — tuition + accommodation + meals + travel + often a stipend
- **Partially funded** — some subset; read exactly which
- **Self-funded** — the student pays; a "place" is not a grant
- **Full Scholarship** vs **Full-Ride**: full scholarship covers tuition only; full-ride covers tuition + housing + food + books + airfare. Students must read which one an offer actually is before accepting or declining another.
- **Application fee** — buys consideration, nothing more. *"To'lov to'lansa, grant beriladi degani emas."*

## By destination `[D]`

### USA
Platform **Common App** (~800 colleges, max 20 per account); the **University of California** system and **Georgetown** are outside it. Documents: transcript, activity list, test scores, essay, teacher + counselor recommendations, School Report, Mid-Year Report in January. Tests: SAT or ACT (not both), TOEFL/IELTS; graduate: GRE, GMAT for business.
Deadline pattern: **Early Action/Decision ≈ 1 November**; **Regular Decision ≈ 1–5 January**; **UC ≈ 30 November**; some public universities to February–March.
Aid vocabulary that matters: **need-blind vs need-aware**, and **"meets full need" vs limited aid**. Need-blind for international students is rare — historically Amherst, Berea, Bowdoin, Dartmouth, Georgetown, Harvard, MIT, Princeton `[V]`.
**CSS Profile** ~$25 first university, ~$16 each additional `[V]`; **fee waivers exist** for family income under $100,000, which covers most Uzbek families — but the automatic waiver often fails for international applicants, so the student should email the university's financial aid office directly and ask for a waiver code or an alternative form. **ISFAA** is free.

### UK
**UCAS**, five choices. **Oxford and Cambridge close 15 October**; most others **15 January**. Postgraduate is program-specific, typically December–February, some rolling into summer.
**Conditional offer** (contingent on IELTS, final grades, or a deposit) vs **unconditional offer**. Some universities issue unconditional only after a deposit. Read the conditions and the date by which they must be met — an unmet condition cancels the offer.

### Italy
Platforms: the university's own portal, national pre-enrolment via **Universitaly**, qualification recognition via **DoV (Declaration of Value)** at the embassy or **CIMEA** online.
Entry tests: **CEnT-S** (replacing English TOLC for science/engineering/economics), **TOLC-I/S/E**, **English Test HUM** for humanities, sometimes SAT. Test policy here is actively changing `[V]` — check the program's own admission page for "CEnT required / CEnT accepted."
Application windows for non-EU students generally **March–July**, many closing April–May `[V]`.
**DoV process `[D]`:** notarised translation of diploma and transcript (Italian or English) → Apostille at the Ministry of Justice (about a day) → submit to the Italian embassy in Tashkent → two to three weeks or longer. Start early; the whole process can take one to two months.
Regional grants: **DSU** (Tuscany and the north — Pisa, Florence, Siena, Bologna, Milan, Politecnico) and **Lazio DiSCo** (Rome — Sapienza, Tor Vergata, Roma Tre, LUISS). Both cover tuition exemption, accommodation, canteen meals and a stipend `[V]`. Terms are re-set every year by the **Bando di Concorso** — the current bando is the only authority. Retention requires roughly 24 ECTS in year one and 30 for renewal `[V]`.
Visa: proof of funds ~€6,947/year, **not blocked** `[V]`, plus accommodation proof, insurance (Schengen, €30,000 coverage), flight booking, and sponsor income documentation — parents' income visible on my.gov.uz.

### Germany
**uni-assist** or the university portal; **DAAD** for scholarships. The most regular deadlines in Europe: **winter semester 15 July**, **summer semester 15 January** (some technical universities 31 May or 15 March).
Bachelor's: check the school-leaving certificate on **anabin.kmk.org**; if it doesn't qualify, Studienkolleg plus Feststellungsprüfung. German B2–C1 (DSH, TestDaF) for German-taught, IELTS 6.0–6.5 for English-taught.
Master's: a recognised degree of at least three years in the field, motivation letter, one to two recommendation letters, transcript, Europass CV.
Visa: **blocked account (Sperrkonto) is mandatory** `[D]` — the amount is set annually `[V]`. This is the key contrast with Italy, where funds are shown but not frozen.

### Rest of Europe
Norway 15 April (autumn) / 1 December (spring); Maastricht 1 February (bachelor's) / 1 April (master's); Finland in January; Stipendium Hungaricum in January (no IELTS required); Türkiye Burslari in February.

## Named scholarship programs `[D]`

**Government:** DAAD and DAAD EPOS (Germany), Eiffel Excellence (France), NL Scholarship and Holland Scholarship, ARES and Belgium Master Mind, Danish Government, NAWA (Poland), Stipendium Hungaricum, Swedish Institute, Irish Government Postgraduate, Finland Government, OeAD (Austria), Romanian Government, Swiss Government Excellence, Slovak and Czech Government, Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters, Chevening (UK), Fulbright (US), Türkiye Burslari.

**Erasmus+ exchange.** Depends on an **inter-institutional agreement between the student's own university and the host**. The first action is always to go to their university's International Office and ask which European universities it has agreements with — everything else follows from that list. Bachelor's from year 2, master's from year 1, PhD for research. Typically IELTS 6.0 or B2, plus a strong GPA. Covers tuition waiver, a monthly stipend and travel `[V]`.

## Uzbek state scholarships `[D]`

**Prezident stipendiyasi** (final-year bachelor's, one per university; master's, one per field) and **nomli stipendiyalar**: Islom Karimov (all fields), Navoiy (humanities), Bobur (history/geography), Beruniy (technical), Ibn Sino (medicine), Pahlavon Mahmud (sport). Full document and eligibility detail is in `uzbek-scholarship-documents.md`.

**El-Yurt Umidi.** Covers tuition in full, living stipend, insurance, visa fees and return airfare. The gating requirement: **an acceptance letter from a QS/THE top-300 university, obtained first** — so the university application comes before the fund application, not after. Three stages: document screening → a test with a hard cut-off (60 is a fail where 61 passes; "almost" does not exist here) → an interview before ministry and fund experts whose central question is why the state should spend this money on this person. Obligation to return and work three to five years in a state body or higher education institution. Country-specific tuition ceilings exist; anything above is paid by the student `[V]`. Strategic note from the corpus: a top-300 university in Germany, Japan, Italy, South Korea or China is usually easier to get into and cheaper than the US or UK, which suits the fund's own limits.

**"Mard o'g'lon"** — males 14–30, deadline 1 March `[V]`, an 18-item document folder including tavsifnoma, obyektivka, 2 taqdimnoma, 2 tavsiyanoma, 4–5 fikrnoma, 1–2 taqriz, achievements list with proofs, media publications, work samples, passport copies, parents' documents, mahalla certificate and photos.

## Short programs, forums and internships `[D]`

Recurring shapes worth knowing:
- **Kectil Program** — ages 17–26, developing countries, 12 online modules, free. The entire selection is a set of 15 essay questions (in `application-questions.md`).
- **UNICEF Internship** — 6–26 weeks, minimum 18, enrolled or graduated within the last two years, fluency in English/French/Spanish, no close relatives employed at UNICEF. Cover letter is the core document.
- **YPIP** (American Councils, Uzbekistan) — 3rd/4th-year business and STEM students, citizen and resident of Uzbekistan, English test, then interviews with two potential host companies. **Plagiarism in the personal essay disqualifies the whole application.**
- **Aspire Leaders** — Harvard-affiliated, free, ages 18–29, priority to low-income and first-generation students.
- **Rotary RYLA** — youth leadership forum; often a short video pitch rather than an essay.
- **Erasmus Mundus internships** — no application fee, stipend, travel and accommodation.

## Preparation runway `[D]`

Working backwards from the intake, roughly: choose the specialisation 18–16 months out → shortlist 10–12 universities 16–12 months → gather program requirements and start test prep 12–10 months → sit the exams 10–8 months → prepare documents 5–4 months → interviews 4–3 months → visa documents and grant applications 2–1 months → travel.

The two corollaries worth telling students directly: **take the language test early**, because a retake needs months that a final-year student does not have; and **for master's, top universities open applications at the start of the fourth year and some close mid-year** — so the preparation belongs in the third year, not the fourth.

**Rough weighting of what decides an application:** grades and exams ~30%, motivation letter ~30%, the profile itself ~30%, recommendation letters ~10%. Useful for triage: a student with three days left should spend them on the letter, not the CV.

## Where to search `[D]`

StudyPortals (studyportals.com) · GoAbroad · Study in Europe (studyineurope.eu) · official national portals — the reliable trick is to search "Study in [country]" and take the government result · QS Top Universities and Times Higher Education for rankings · BigFuture (College Board) for US colleges · usnews.com for US rankings · DAAD's program database for Germany · uni-assist for German applications · UCAS for the UK · Universitaly for Italy.

## What to always verify

Deadlines, tuition, stipend amounts, GPA minimums, language-test thresholds, entry-test policy, ranking positions, need-blind status, and whether a scholarship is open to international students at all — several US full-rides are domestic-only or nomination-only. Tell the student which page to open, not just "check the website."
